{% if predict|lower == 'glioma' %}
Description Gliomas are a type of tumor that starts in the glial cells of the brain or spinal cord. They can be low-grade (slow-growing) or high-grade (aggressive and fast-growing), and their impact depends on size, location, and grade.
Causes The exact cause is unknown, but risk factors include genetic mutations, family history of brain tumors, exposure to radiation, and certain inherited syndromes.
Prevention There is no sure way to prevent gliomas, but reducing exposure to harmful radiation, maintaining a healthy lifestyle, and going for regular check-ups may help in early detection.
Treatment Treatment options depend on tumor type, size, and grade. They may include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or a combination of these approaches.
Diet Adopt a nutrient-rich diet with plenty of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins. Foods rich in omega-3 fatty acids may be beneficial.
Physical Activity Engage in light to moderate exercise, such as walking, yoga, or stretching, as tolerated. Physical activity supports overall health and recovery.
Body Condition Maintaining a healthy weight and good physical condition can improve treatment outcomes. Patients may require rehabilitation to maintain strength.
Affected Age Gliomas can occur at any age but are more common in adults between 45 and 65 years old.
Additional Info Gliomas can range from slow-growing benign-like tumors to highly aggressive forms such as glioblastoma. Early diagnosis and multidisciplinary treatment improve outcomes.
{% elif predict|lower == 'meningioma' %}
Description Meningiomas are typically slow-growing tumors that arise from the meninges, the protective membranes covering the brain and spinal cord. Most are benign, but some may be atypical or malignant.
Causes The exact cause is not always known. Risk factors include genetic mutations, previous exposure to ionizing radiation, hormonal influences, and family history.
Prevention There is no guaranteed prevention, but reducing exposure to unnecessary radiation, maintaining overall brain health, and regular medical check-ups may lower risk.
Treatment Treatment may involve surgical removal, radiation therapy, or careful monitoring for small, asymptomatic tumors. Treatment depends on size, location, and symptoms.
Diet A nutrient-rich diet with plenty of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins supports overall brain health. Limit processed foods and refined sugars.
Physical Activity Moderate physical activity such as walking, yoga, or swimming helps improve circulation and support recovery after treatment.
Body Condition Maintaining a healthy weight and balanced lifestyle can reduce risk factors. Patients should follow individualized medical and rehabilitation plans.
Affected Age Meningiomas most commonly occur in adults between 40 and 70 years, and are more frequent in women than men. They are rare in children.
Additional Info While most meningiomas are non-cancerous, their location can cause neurological symptoms such as headaches, vision problems, or seizures. Regular MRI scans are used to monitor growth.
{% elif predict|lower == 'pituitary' %}
Description Pituitary tumors are abnormal growths that develop in the pituitary gland, a small gland at the base of the brain that controls many body functions through hormone production. Most are benign but can affect hormone balance.
Causes The exact cause is often unknown. Genetic mutations, hereditary conditions such as Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 1 (MEN1), or abnormal cell growth may contribute.
Prevention There is no guaranteed way to prevent pituitary tumors. Regular health check-ups, early detection of hormonal imbalances, and monitoring for genetic risk factors help in early diagnosis.
Treatment Treatment options may include surgery to remove the tumor, medications to regulate hormone levels, or radiation therapy. The treatment plan depends on the tumor's size, type, and impact.
Diet Maintain a nutrient-rich diet with plenty of vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and lean proteins. Reducing processed foods and excess sugar supports overall health.
Physical Activity Engage in moderate physical activities such as walking, yoga, or cycling. Exercise can improve overall health and reduce stress, based on medical advice.
Body Condition Regular monitoring of weight, hormone levels, and general health is essential. Pituitary tumors can sometimes cause weight gain, fatigue, or other hormonal changes.
Affected Age Pituitary tumors can occur at any age but are more commonly diagnosed in adults between 30 and 60 years. Children can also be affected in rare cases.
Additional Info Although most pituitary tumors are non-cancerous, they can cause serious symptoms by pressing on nearby brain structures or disrupting hormone production. Regular MRI scans and hormone tests are important for management.
